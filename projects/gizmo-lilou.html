How might we capture the presence of a living being through mechanical interaction design? Bringing warmth and curiosity to mechanical form.

Overview

Lilou is an interactive robotic companion that explores how motion, sensing, and timing can evoke emotion in human-robot interaction. Developed using Bela IDE and a variety of optical, touch, and audio sensors, the project mimics the behaviour of a real dog by performing small “tricks,” reacting to inputs, and expressing different emotional states.

The concept originated as a homage to a teammate’s dog, Lilou, and evolved into an exploration of how mechanical form and motion could communicate the attachment. Beyond entertainment, Lilou expected to be served as educational way to encourage children to understand animal emotions and the importance of empathy.

INTERACTION LOOP The behaviour model was structured around three core emotions states - happy, hungry, and scared - with a substate of excited nested within happiness. Instead of simple one-to-one responses, Lilou follows a cycle of emotional states to possess a holistic personality.
FUNCTION DEVELOPMENT Along with Bela IDE, servo motors, QRE optical sensor, trill touch sensors, and microphone were incorporated as an external environment stimuli.
  • Servo motors to simulate subtle sitting and paw lifting motions
  • Optical sensor embedded in nose to simulate a sense of smell
  • Microphone to detect audio decible for the trigger for the scared state
