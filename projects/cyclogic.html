How might we turn data-driven insight into real business value for urban cyclists? Creating a system that connects market understanding with user experience.

Overview

Cyclogic is a mobile navigation solution tailored for urban cyclists who commute daily within dynamic city environments. The project explored how user experience design and business strategy can together create meaningful value in a growing mobility market.

Rooted in market analysis and opportunity validation, the project integrated app feature development with business modelling. This included: market size assumption, business model, profit calculations, channel strategy, assumption validation, and more.

I contributed end-to-end market research, hypothesis testing, and collaborative UI development to ensure the concept was both user-centered and market-viable.

Problem

Initially, the team started with four broad areas of: Personal safety, Bike condition, Navigation, and Bike theft. The initial survey with 172 responses suggested that the common issue arise within navigation and unpredictable road and infrastructure on its way.



We then narrowed down the ideas along with market and secondary research, and held in-depth survey against 23 cyclists, resulting in following insights.

Inefficient Navigation Experience
Most cycling apps required users to stop mid-ride to recheck directions, breaking flow and focus.
Unreliable Route Suggestions
Suggested routes often led to non-cyclable or unsafe paths, resulting in re-routes from unexpected changes.
Lack of Personalised Familiarity
Cyclists felt safer when riding familiar paths, yet most algorithms optimised only for speed, not comfort or personal familiarity.
SYSTEM DIAGRAM Created a system diagram aligning both user-facing and back-end elements, defining three integrated flows:

1. Personal data-driven nagivation: learning from past rides to tailor recommendations.
2. Community data loop: real-time updates and alerts shared among cyclists.
3. Live environmental feedback: instant road condition and incident reports enhancing route reliability.
APP INTERFACE App interfaces were explored to incorporate the system diagram into navigation journey.

1. Familiarity-based routing: proposes the algorithm calculating similarity to a cyclist’s previously saved or frequently used paths.
2. Crowdsourced data: evokes cyclist's sense of community.
3. In-ride user interface: intuitive in-ride interface where large map visuals were replaced by a dynamic arrow system
