How might we design a single tool to adapt across diverse athletic grip needs? Iterating from prototype to product with purpose and precision.

Overview

SkillGrip was developed as part of a full-cycle industrial and mechatronic product design project, from early research through to prototyping and DFMA-level (Design for Manufacturing and Assembly) refinement.

Broadly started from the needs of sports players, the project landed on a modular grip trainer that adapts to different sports and athletic training contexts. With interchangeable handles based on three key grip classifications - bar, ball, and crimp - the system supports tailored grip training for users ranging from hobbyists to professional athletes.

* Technogym were not engaged in any consultancy or collaborative capacity with this project and the outcome is in no way endorsed by them. Any publicity is limited to personal and academic use.

Problem

Starting broadly at sports player's need, the team held an interview and survey against hobbyists - professional level of athelets. As a result, we identified the following specific points to address.

Three Main Grip Types
Through synthesis, three distinct grip types of ball, bar, and pinch were identified, each requiring different contact areas and training patterns.
Limited and High Stiffness
Existing grip trainers often suffer from limited adjustability and high stiffness, causing strain or injury during use.
Beyond Grip Strength
Effective performance training requires integrated wrist and forearm strengthening, not just isolated grip improvement.

MECHANISM The mechanical system to control the dynamic resistance was explored. Leading screw method was chosen over direct motor roll and rack and pinion method due to its high precision and ability to prevent backdriving.

Then electronic controls were integrated with Arduino C++, incorporating button inputs to motor rotations and LED indicators. The motor rotation adjusts the location of the lead screw, increasing or decreaasing tension to match user input.
PROTOTYPING & ITERATION The CAD model was developed in Fusion 360 with full consideration for manufacturability and assembly efficiency.
  • Electronics proximity that does not interfere with other motions
  • Ribs, bosses, and fillets for internal strength and tolerance management
  • Snap-fit, press-fit and joining techniques for modular assembly
  • Horizontal motor assembly and z-axis final assembly
BRANDING Technogym with the value of spreading the wellness lifestyle is selected as branding extension. To align with "Skill" product line, visual and tactile cues were incorporated across form and finish.
